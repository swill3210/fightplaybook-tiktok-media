# The Fight Playbook: combat mechanics and visual motion master skill (Sean's spec)

Recorded from Sean's message. Lists kept complete; wording condensed only where it was explanatory.

1. Objective: a visual motion dictionary (not only text) for boxing, kickboxing, Muay Thai, MMA, wrestling, BJJ and judo: verified reference footage, sequential illustrations, character poses, anatomical references, movement demos, preserving the approved 2D characters.
2. Universal mechanics: natural joint limits; consistent limb lengths and proportions; realistic balance and centre of gravity; correct foot placement and ground contact; synchronised shoulder, hip and torso; realistic weight distribution; no floating limbs, duplicated hands or disconnected joints; no characters passing through each other; consistent orientation; never bend elbows, knees or wrists impossibly; realistic gravity, momentum and interaction.
3. Boxing: stances and footwork (orthodox, southpaw, high guard, low guard, peekaboo, Philly shell, forward and backward, lateral, pivoting, circling, angle changes, step and slide). Punches: jab, cross (visible torso and hip rotation), lead hook (curved horizontal), rear hook, lead uppercut, rear uppercut, overhand (loops over the guard), body punches visibly distinct from head punches; combinations and transitions. Defence: slips left and right, bob and weave, ducking, rolling, pullbacks, parrying, high guard block, body block, shoulder roll, defensive pivots, distance management.
4. Kickboxing and Muay Thai: front kick, teep, roundhouse, low kick, body kick, high kick, side kick, back kick, spinning back kick, hook kick, question mark kick, switch kick, axe kick (for each: silhouette, supporting leg, kicking leg trajectory, body orientation, recovery). Knees and elbows: straight knee, curved knee, switch knee, horizontal elbow, rising elbow, downward elbow, spinning elbow. Defence: checking, kick evasion, framing, guard adjustments, clinch positioning, controlled disengagement. Controlled sporting demos, no graphic injuries.
5. Wrestling and MMA takedowns: single leg, double leg, high crotch, body lock, ankle pick, inside trip, outside trip, foot sweep, snapdown, arm drag, duck under, mat return, sprawl, takedown defence, cage wrestling, standing grappling: starting relationship, elevation changes, transition poses, controlled landings, realistic contact and balance.
6. Judo, throws and slams: hip throws, shoulder throws, foot sweeps, inside reaps, outside reaps, sacrifice throws, controlled suplex style, controlled body lock throws, mat returns, breakfalls; controlled environments with mats; no exaggerated impacts or injuries.
7. BJJ: positions (closed, open, half, butterfly guard; full mount; side control; north south; back control; knee on belly; turtle; standing clinch). Movements (bridging, shrimping, hip escapes, technical stand ups, guard retention, guard passing, sweeps, transitions, mount escapes, back control escapes). Submission recognition (rear naked choke, guillotine, triangle, arm triangle, D'Arce, anaconda, armbar, kimura, americana, omoplata, straight ankle lock, kneebar, heel hook): recognisable positions only, visible tap and immediate release; never harmful pressure, injury mechanics or unconsciousness.
8. Visual dictionary: A striking reference sheets (approved characters, phases, body position, trajectories, orientation, multiple cameras, correct vs incorrect); B sequential sheets (start, intermediate, action, completion, recovery, continuity); C grappling position sheets (relative positions, orientation, limb placement, ground contact, no merging or extra limbs); D character turnaround sheets (front, rear, left, right, three quarter, overhead, low angle, neutral, boxing stance, grappling, common poses; exact clothing, colours, branding, proportions, shading); E skeletal and pose library (skeletons, pose estimation, joint maps, OpenPose compatible, silhouettes, interaction references, joint limits; explore ControlNet, pose estimation, LoRA); F motion reference clips (reputable footage catalogued with source, instructor, technique, timestamps; internal reference only, never republished without permission).
9. Per technique entry: names and aliases, category, visual description, start posture, intermediate poses, end posture, limb positions, orientation, balance and weight, trajectory, interaction, common animation mistakes, recommended camera angles, approved reference images, sequential sheets, verified footage, image prompt, negative prompt, pose control references, QC requirements, searchable tags.
10. Research and verification: reputable coaches and organisations; GitHub (motion datasets, mocap, pose estimation, skeletal animation, biomechanics, rigging, pose controlled generation, combat animation); MediaPipe, OpenPose, ControlNet, Blender, ComfyUI; cross check multiple credible sources; never invent footage or claim unverified accuracy.
11. Camera library per movement: front, rear, left profile, right profile, three quarter, overhead, low angle, close up, over the shoulder, wide, first person, cinematic tracking; camera changes never reverse stances, switch limbs or alter the movement; keep the 2D aesthetic.
12. ElevenLabs integration: identify the movement in the narration; retrieve mechanics reference, sequential reference, character reference, camera, pose control; generate; inspect; correct or regenerate; approve before use. Identify the exact underlying model; evaluate alternatives only with measurable improvement; never trade accuracy for creativity.
13. Integration with the visual standard: a new still or meaningful change every 1.5 s, cinematic angles, kinetic typography, animated numbers, countdowns, 2.5D parallax, motion graphics, instructional overlays, split screens, freeze frames, slow motion, synced narration and SFX; keep tutorial structure and exact 2D designs; do not interrupt continuous demos just for pacing.
14. Mandatory QC rejects: extra or missing limbs, floating hands, disconnected joints, distorted arms, backward elbows or knees, unnatural wrists, wrong punching or kicking trajectories, impossible grappling, clipping, wrong orientation, unintended stance switch, unnatural rotation, inconsistent proportions, wrong ground contact, unrealistic gravity, movement contradicting narration, design inconsistency, camera changes breaking continuity; compare against references; review the whole render including transitions; correct every error.
15. Structure: skill `fight-playbook-combat-mechanics/SKILL.md`; library folders Boxing, Kickboxing, Muay Thai, MMA, Wrestling, Judo, BJJ, Anatomy and Biomechanics, Character Turnaround Sheets, Sequential Motion References, Skeletal and Pose References, Grappling Position References, Verified Reference Footage, Cinematic Camera References, Image Generation Prompts, Negative Prompts, Quality Control, Research and Sources; indexes in Obsidian; skill files, metadata, prompts and code in Git; large media separate; CLAUDE.md must require consulting it before any combat still or animation.
16. Continuous learning: new technique means research, verify, document, build sheets, add, validate before use; every animation error Sean finds is logged with cause and correction; temporary scripts never overwrite permanent skills or character rules; do not modify TikTok or promo workflows.
17. Initial deliverables: skill file; organised dictionary; approved turnaround sheets; sample striking, kicking, wrestling and grappling sheets; sample sequential references; verified source database; integration report (ElevenLabs and GitHub skills); 30 s demo. Clearly separate completed from pending; never call the library complete before its references are created and verified.
