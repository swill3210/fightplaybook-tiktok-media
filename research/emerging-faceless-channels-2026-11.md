# Emerging Faceless Channels: Explosive-Growth Scan (Nov 2026)

**Data date:** 2026-11-25 · **Source:** vidIQ (live YouTube data) · **Credits used:** 130 (about 1,730 left)

**Method:**
1. 17 channel searches. Filters: created on or after **2026-08-27** (last 90 days), long-form, 150K–500K+ total views, ≤60 videos. Topics covered: history, science, fiction/animation, finance, health, religion/myth, crime, nature, nostalgia, survival, geography, sleep, plus 5 unfiltered searches. That surfaced **678 channels**, of which **~100 were English long-form** candidates.
2. Two breakout-video sweeps across 50 shortlisted channels, counting **long-form videos with 100K+ views**. These counts are minimums: one sweep was capped at 100 results, and a few channels weren't in the second sweep.
3. Seven format-saturation checks. Each looked at who else (small and large channels) is winning in that format right now.
4. Thumbnails viewed directly for 10 representative videos.

**Labels:**
- **[V] Verified** — straight from vidIQ.
- **[E] Estimate** — my calculation, labelled as such.
- **[A] Assumption or judgement.**

**Excluded on purpose:**
- second channels of established creators (SSundeePlus, Caylus IRL, FoltynGaming)
- Shorts-first channels (most of the 678)
- music channels and re-uploads
- channels that aren't faceless
- non-English channels (their ad rates are much lower)

## 3. Copyright, policy and monetization risks found [V where shown]

- ⛔ **Elias Wild:** the thumbnail shows a **real Fox News host's likeness and the FOX NEWS logo** with "BREAKING" [V, seen directly]. That's impersonation plus trademark misuse, so expect strikes or termination. Don't copy.
- ⚠ **Final Coordinates and its imitators:** thumbnails use what look like real young women's photos with "KARMA K*LLED THEM" [V, seen]. Several titles describe deaths that may be fabricated ("3 Girl Scouts vanished on the Appalachian Trail in 2019") [A, unverified]. Presenting invented deaths as real, with real-looking faces, risks **misinformation and defamation** claims. Only workable with **verified, documented cases** and respectful framing.
- ⚠ **America with Amos, One Room Survival, Dana Schofield (@danaschofield*vet*), Hank Mercer, Frugal Linda, "I'm 70…" titles:** these use AI "presenter" personas, often elderly or veterans [V for the Amos and One Room Survival thumbnails]. Undisclosed fake personas with claimed credentials ("Army supply sergeant says…") are risky under YouTube's misleading-content and altered-content disclosure rules. Retail logos (Walmart) in thumbnails are a trademark gray area.
- ⚠ **Born History** (Trump / "history says this happens next"): political content attracts advertiser limits and volatile algorithm treatment.
- ⚠ **The Diaspora Files:** grew very fast (36.8K subs in 24 days). The subject is sensitive (Hitler, antisemitism), so expect heavy comment moderation and some ad limits.
- **DVD Rewind** (classic TV): risk of copyright claims if it uses show clips or stills.
- **All AI-heavy formats:** YouTube's 2025 "inauthentic content" monetization rule (mass-produced, repetitive content) is the main long-term risk. Original scripts, sourcing and visible editorial judgement are the defence.

## 4. The five strongest opportunities

These are picked for **evidence of several small, new channels winning + room left before saturation + ad rates + whether AI agents can automate it.** A channel's views alone weren't enough.

### #1 — Comparative economic history with animated maps ("Why did A get rich but B never did?") — **84/100**
- **Evidence [V]:** at least 9 channels under 15K subscribers have breakout videos in the last 3 months, including 1.5M (Pinpoint Maps, 3K subs), 1.2M (Gribnax, 3.6K subs) and 1.8M (Fortune of Nations, 14.5K subs). A 427-subscriber channel got 147K. Small channels are beating Versed (206K subs) in the same results.
- **Why it stands out [A]:** finance and economics audiences pay premium ad rates (est. $5–10 RPM). The topic is evergreen. The visuals are clean animated maps that an agent can generate reliably (map data plus motion templates). Scripts reward real research, which protects against the inauthentic-content rule.
- **How to be first:** most videos cover the same pairs (US vs Latin America, the Koreas, Israel vs neighbours, Haiti vs Dominican Republic). Pairs that look uncovered: Botswana vs Zimbabwe, Chile vs Argentina, Estonia vs Latvia, Vietnam vs Philippines, Costa Rica vs Nicaragua, Kazakhstan vs Uzbekistan, Uruguay vs Paraguay, Ireland vs Portugal, plus US state pairs (Minnesota vs Wisconsin, Colorado vs New Mexico).
- **Production:** 10–25 min, 1–2/week. **Cost [E]:** $15–40/video in tools (voice, map rendering, music) plus agent time. **AI reproducibility:** high.

### #2 — Sub-national megaprojects and infrastructure (province, state, city) — **78/100**
- **Evidence [V]:** channels with 992–3K subscribers are getting 130K–221K views on province- or city-specific projects (Canada Beyond, Spruce and Steel, BC Backstory, Greenwich Files). Canada Rebuilt and Britain 2050 are growing at +87% (30 days) and +73% (7 days).
- **Why it stands out [A]:** Canadian, UK and Australian audiences have high ad rates. Local pride drives shares and comments. The national level is owned by The B1M, but **individual provinces, states and cities are wide open.** Facts come from public government and engineering documents, which makes it easy to source and research-heavy enough to look original.
- **Risks:** nationalist rage-bait titles ("Canada's REVENGE", "END America's grip"); avoid them. AI renders of projects must not be passed off as real photos.
- **Production:** 15–30 min, 2/week. **Cost [E]:** $15–35/video. **AI reproducibility:** high (renders, maps, document callouts).

### #3 — "Forgotten skills and how life used to work" nostalgia for older audiences — **76/100**
- **Evidence [V]:** Boys To Men (1.6K subs, 3 videos over 100K, +236% views in 30 days, one video at a 225x breakout), plus the earlier verified research cluster: Lost America 409K, Forgotten Appalachian Skills 259K, Britain As It Was 258K, Forgotten American Farm & Country Life 310K, plus the military-life and nostalgia channels.
- **Why it stands out [A]:** a 55+ audience in the US, UK and Canada brings top ad rates, long watch sessions (20–40 min) and very high comment rates (people share memories). The visuals are AI period illustrations, which avoids copyright problems. **Specific sub-genres are still open:** a gendered angle ("Boys To Men"), trades (railroad men, milkmen, switchboard operators), and regional angles (Appalachia, the Prairies, Northern England).
- **Production:** 20–35 min, 2–3/week. **Cost [E]:** $20–40/video. **AI reproducibility:** high. Use the Vietnam-era research findings here: sourcing, no fake personas, rotating title formulas.

### #4 — Long-form "Entire history of [country/empire/topic] in N minutes" — **74/100**
- **Evidence [V]:** Ziro History has 12 videos over 100K in 53 days and +174% views in 30 days. CountryCorns has 3 (1.1M on its best) but has **stopped growing**. Prima Historian has 2 out of 8 videos.
- **Why it stands out [A]:** 35–55 minute videos earn several mid-roll ads each. Search demand is evergreen ("history of X"). It's very automatable (maps, period illustrations, timelines).
- **Why not #1:** legacy history channels already own most country names. **The way in is the under-covered subjects:** Central Asian khanates, African kingdoms (Kongo, Great Zimbabwe, Ashanti), Pacific nations, the Baltic states, Caucasus states, individual US states.
- **Production:** 30–50 min, 1–2/week. **Cost [E]:** $25–60/video (long voiceover). **AI reproducibility:** high; fact-checking is the bottleneck.

### #5 — Wilderness cautionary documentaries, **verified real cases only** — **70/100**
- **Evidence [V]:** Final Coordinates has 14 videos over 100K in 55 days, +290% views in 30 days, and **2.3–3.2% engagement** (very high). Small imitators are also winning: The Last Trail (3.5K subs, 148K), Cliff Hollis (8.7K subs, 355K), James Veindrich, Disaster Archives, The Last Climb.
- **Why it stands out [A]:** 25-minute videos with strong watch time and high engagement. The audience is outdoors, hiking, true crime and US-heavy.
- **Why it's only #5:** copycats are arriving fast, and the leading format appears to dramatize or invent cases with real-looking faces. **Our version only works with real, documented incidents** (park service reports, coroner and news records), respectful framing, no real photos of victims in thumbnails, and an angle like "lessons from the case". That's slower but defensible. **AI reproducibility:** medium-high (research is the constraint).

### Deliberately NOT recommended despite big numbers
- **Animated zombie survival** and **AI drama audio stories:** saturated, low ad rates.
- **Blackout prep lists:** saturated, seasonal, built on personas.
- **"[State] is hiding something":** saturated.
- **Invasive-species clickbait:** saturated, and its leader misuses Fox News branding.
- **Day One Films:** about 3-minute videos, so little long-form ad revenue despite 14M views.
- **BBTV NEW / CountryCorns / Mr. Doomsday:** stalled. Proof that early spikes don't always last.

- **"Mo. views"** is a lifetime average, not true 30-day views. vidIQ's growth percentages are the better recent-trend signal.
- **"Faceless" status** was flagged as unknown (`isFaceless: null`) for most channels. I inferred it from thumbnails, titles and descriptions [A].
- **Some 100K+ counts are minimums** because one sweep was capped at 100 results.
- **Non-English opportunities weren't explored in depth.** Ad rates are lower, though some (Spanish, Portuguese) have far less competition. That could be a follow-up scan.
