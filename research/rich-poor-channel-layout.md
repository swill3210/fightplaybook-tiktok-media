# Channel Layout: Comparative Economic History with Animated Maps

**Format:** "Why did A get rich but B didn't?" · **Data date:** 2026-11-25
**Labels:** [V] = verified via vidIQ · [E] = estimate · [A] = my recommendation

---

## 1. Positioning

**Working concept [A]:** *Two places, one question: why did their fortunes split?*
Every video compares two neighbouring or similar places with very different outcomes. It explains the split with real data and ends with a clear verdict.

**How we beat the field [A]:** the current winners are often light on evidence (see section 5). We win on three things:
1. **Uncovered pairs.** The leaders keep remaking US vs Latin America, the Koreas, Haiti vs Dominican Republic and Israel vs its neighbours.
2. **Data on screen.** Show the actual numbers, with sources in the corner of the frame.
3. **A fair verdict.** "Here's what mattered most, and what didn't."

**Name ideas** (check they're available): *Two Fates* · *Split Fortunes* · *The Wealth Line* · *Border of Fortune*. Your call. Don't use names close to "Fortune of Nations", "Atlas Economics" or "Rich Map, Poor Map".

**Audience [A]:** English-speaking, mostly US/UK/Canada/Australia, 25–55, interested in economics, geography and history. This is a high-paying ad audience.

## 3. Video template (12–18 minutes, ~1,900–2,700 words at ~150 wpm)

**Length:** the verified winners run 8:36–16:45 [V]: Pinpoint Maps 8:36, Fortune of Nations 9:09–10:09, Atlas Economics 15:40–16:45. **Target 12–16 minutes:** long enough for mid-roll ads, short enough to keep people watching.

| Time | Section | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:30 | **Cold-open contrast** | Two numbers side by side: "In 1960, A and B were equally poor. Today, A is 6× richer." Map splits into two colours. Ask the question in one sentence. |
| 0:30–1:00 | **Promise + stakes** | "The answer isn't the one most people give." Name the obvious explanation you'll test (oil, colonisation, culture) and promise a verdict. |
| 1:00–2:30 | **Starting line** | Geography: terrain, ports, rivers, climate. Shown with a 3D relief map and labelled features. |
| 2:30–10:30 | **4–5 factor chapters** (~1.5–2 min each) | Each factor follows the same order: claim → historical moment (dated) → data chart → map animation → "how much did this matter?" meter (low/med/high). |
| 10:30–12:30 | **The turning point** | The single decision or event where the paths split, told as a short story with dates. |
| 12:30–14:00 | **Verdict** | Rank the factors. Say what *didn't* matter (myth-busting). Fair, no politics. |
| 14:00–14:40 | **Where they're headed** | Current trend with latest data. |
| 14:40–15:00 | **Call to action** | "Which pair should we do next? Comment two places." This feeds the topic pipeline. |

**Rules for the Script agent:**
- One question per video, answered.
- Every number has a year and a source.
- No country insults or hidden political agendas.
- Rotate the hook formula ("Same X, two fates" / "In [year] they were equal" / "Why does [rich] have [n]× more…").

## 4. Visual system

**Thumbnails [V, seen directly]:**
- Fortune of Nations: split screen of two 3D-relief maps, the rich side glowing green and the poor side brown, with a **red "WHY?" box**.
- Pinpoint Maps: one satellite-style map with the border visible, coloured labels ("🇭🇹 POOREST" / "🇩🇴 6× RICHER"), and a question headline ("SAME ISLAND?").

**Our thumbnail template [A]:**
- Two places on one map, rich side saturated green or gold, poor side desaturated.
- One number badge ("6× RICHER").
- A headline of 3 words or fewer, as a question.
- Flags as small icons only.

**Video visuals [A]:**
- Base layer: 3D relief maps from Natural Earth data (public domain) or hillshade renders, styled in a consistent palette.
- Animations:
  - border glow
  - colour fills over time (GDP per capita by decade)
  - arrows (trade, migration)
  - city dots sized by population
- Charts: animated line charts (GDP per capita, both countries, same axis) and bar comparisons.
- Period scenes: AI illustrations in one consistent style (e.g. painterly, muted), **disclosed as AI**, used for historical moments only. Never fake photos.
- Visual change every 3–5 seconds. Source caption bottom-left ("World Bank, 2024").

## 5. What the top competitor actually does

From a vidIQ watch of Fortune of Nations' [1.8M-view video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GD7x6sXMnNI) (9:41) [watch-tool report]:

**Hook:**
- It opens on a **current news peg**: silver hit a record price this year.
- Then a paradox: the mountain that produced half the world's silver is in Bolivia, one of the poorest countries.
- Then a reversal: "300 years ago, this side of the map looked like the winner."
- **Copy the structure (news peg → paradox → reversal), not the words.**

**Structure:** 8 sections, factor by factor:
1. paradox
2. starting conditions
3. resource curse
4. where the wealth went
5. the North's strategy (land, schools, votes)
6. empire breakups
7. present-day scorecard
8. one-line thesis

**Visuals:**
- 3D relief map with a parchment texture.
- Glowing borders, animated trade and migration paths, coin-stack charts, a **running scorecard**.
- **Cartoon flags with faces and boxing gloves** (the "versus" game framing).
- No stock footage, all motion graphics. A visual change every 3–5 seconds.

**Audio:**
- Human-sounding baritone at ~140–160 wpm.
- Low orchestral bed.
- Sound effects: coin clinks, paper, whooshes, cannons.

**Weaknesses we can beat:**
1. **No sources cited** anywhere.
2. **A single cause** (resource curse) presented as destiny.
3. **18 countries lumped together** as "Latin America".
4. Subscribe CTA at 3:10 says "videos take a long time to make", which is weak.

**→ Adopt:** news-peg hook, running scorecard, versus framing, 3–5 second visual pace.
**→ Improve:** on-screen sources, multiple causes weighted in the verdict, specific pairs instead of whole regions.

## 7. Cadence and launch plan

- **Weeks 1–2:**
  - build the map and chart templates once (the biggest one-off job)
  - produce videos 1–3 and hold them as private drafts for your review
- **Weeks 3–8:** **2 videos/week** (Tue/Fri, ~1pm ET), working through the topic bank in order.
- **Rules:**
  - no more than 2 videos in a row in the same region
  - after every 6 videos, swap the bottom performer's format for a new hook
  - use viewer comment requests as the topic source from video 8 on
- **Kill / pivot rule:** if videos 1–8 average under 5K views after 7 days each, re-test thumbnails and pairs before producing more.

## 8. Money and costs [E]

- **Ad rate (RPM):** $5–10 for economics/geography with a US/UK audience [E].
- **Example:**
  - 8 videos/month × 30K average views = 240K views/month, which is **$1.2–2.4K/month** once monetized.
  - One breakout (the field has several 1M+ videos [V]) can add $5–15K.
- **Monetization threshold:** 1,000 subscribers + 4,000 watch hours. At 12–16 minutes per video, that's roughly 20K–30K total views.
- **Costs:**
  - tools ~$80–200/month (voice, image generation, music licence, vidIQ)
  - template setup is a one-off of ~20–40 hours, done by agents with your review
  - ~1 hour of your review per video

## 9. Risks

- **Saturation:** at least 9 small channels are already in this format [V]. The window is months, not years. Speed and unclaimed pairs matter.
- **Accuracy:** economics is contested. Oversimplifying invites "well actually" pile-ons. The fact-check step and on-screen sources are non-negotiable.
- **Politics:** some pairs (Israel, Russia, Venezuela) attract heated comments and some ad limits. Keep the tone neutral.
- **Mass-produced-content rule:** templated AI content can be demonetized. Original data analysis, a clear verdict and sourcing are what make each video defensibly original.
