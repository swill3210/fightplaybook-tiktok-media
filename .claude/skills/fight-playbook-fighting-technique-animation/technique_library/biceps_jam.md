# Biceps jam (shoulder stop, arm jam against a haymaker)
Created Nov 23 2026 for tt_haymaker_counters (3 Ways to Counter Someone Who Throws Nothing but Haymakers). Source: FIGHTING_TECHNIQUE_ANIMATION.md sections 3, 15, 16; Combat Judo "THE STOP" (US Army FM 21 150); Athlon Outdoors shoulder stop.

**Alternative names:** biceps jam, shoulder stop, THE STOP, arm jam, frame on the biceps.

**Starting stance (orthodox):** left foot forward, gloves at the cheeks, close range; the attacker is winding up a wide haymaker.

**Arm and leg positioning:** the LEAD arm shoots STRAIGHT out at shoulder height like a stiff jab (elbow soft, never locked), fist into the inside of the swinging arm's upper arm / shoulder pocket. Rear glove stays on the rear cheek. Step in as it lands.

**Trajectory:** straight line from the chin to the upper arm, early in the haymaker's arc (before it builds power).

**Hip and shoulder rotation:** small lead shoulder turn, lead shoulder up by the chin.

**Target:** biceps just above the elbow crease, or the shoulder pocket where shoulder meets chest, of the SWINGING arm. Never the other arm.

**Recovery:** follow straight into strikes, clinch or the grab; rear hand never leaves the face.

**Opponent reaction:** the swinging arm stops out wide, the attacker's rotation stalls, chin left open.

**Common animation mistakes (seen in tt_haymaker_counters):**
- With anatomical left/right wording ("partner's right arm"), the model drew the jam on the attacker's OTHER (hanging) arm 5 times out of 5 (hm_jam_a/b, hm_jam2_a/b/c), with the raised arm on the far side.
- Fix that worked 3/3: describe the swinging arm by IMAGE position ("the arm CLOSEST to the main character, on the RIGHT side of the partner's body in the image"), say the fist presses into "THAT SAME RAISED SWINGING ARM", and describe the meeting as a T shape. Approved still: tt_haymaker_counters raw/hm_jam3_c.jpg.

**Best camera angles:** side or high angle side; the T shape of the two arms must be visible.
